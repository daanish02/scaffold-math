Eigenvectors & eigenvalues

The directions a transformation doesn't rotate away from — only stretches or shrinks.

Apply a matrix to almost any vector, and it rotates — it points somewhere new. But for certain special vectors, the transformation only stretches or compresses them; the direction stays exactly the same. These are the eigenvectors of the matrix, and the amount they're stretched by is the corresponding eigenvalue.

Common pitfall Not every matrix has real eigenvectors — a pure rotation, for instance, turns every vector, so its eigenvalues come out complex.

Why this matters later

PCA finds the eigenvectors of a covariance matrix to identify directions of greatest variance. Google's original PageRank found the eigenvector of a web-link matrix. And diagonalizing a matrix — rewriting it purely in terms of its eigenvectors — is what makes repeated transformations (like ) cheap to compute. 

import numpy as np

A = np.array([[1.6, 0.4], [0.4, 0.9]])
eigenvalues, eigenvectors = np.linalg.eig(A)
